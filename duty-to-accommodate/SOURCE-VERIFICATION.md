# Duty to Accommodate — official-source verification and limits
**Research checked:** 12 November 2026
**Scope:** Canadian federal public service, with priority to the core public administration. This file documents the source basis of the public, independent DTA guide. It does not certify legal compliance or replace a review of the employee's applicable employer instruments and collective agreement.

## Source hierarchy
1. **Binding legislation and regulations:** These control legal rights and duties within their application.
2. **Binding administrative Treasury Board instruments:** Applicable only to institutions in scope, and subject to the text and effective dates.
3. **Official implementation guidance:** Provides procedural detail, recommended practice and interpretation. It is not automatically law.
4. **Departmental reports and survey data:** Evidence of administrative practice and employee experiences, not proof of individual wrongdoing.

## Rules not to overstate

- **"You must get a medical note": false as a universal statement.** A note may be reasonably required, but first consider dialogue and already available information.
- **"An employer can demand the diagnosis": generally false.** Information about restrictions and functional abilities may be required, with narrow exceptions for genuine safety needs.
- **"Every medical note is paid for by the employer": unsupported.** The 2019 federal source specifically distinguishes the two model forms and says departments should *consider* reimbursing the functional-abilities fee. The CHRC recommends employer funding for an appropriate independent examination, but this is not the same process.
- **"A doctor alone can dictate the exact accommodation": too broad.** Clinical information informs work needs; the employer must assess reasonable measures in collaboration with the worker, up to undue hardship.
- **"Employees can refuse all requests for relevant evidence": false.** A refusal to cooperate with reasonable information needs can affect the duty-to-accommodate assessment.
- **"An accommodation request belongs in every HR file": false.** Both the manager guide and the privacy notice require strict confidentiality and restricted access.
- **"A changed manager always requires a new medical assessment": false.** TBS guidance specifically discourages this without a real change that makes updated information necessary.
- **"An ombuds must be the first contact": false.** TBS and CHRC start with workplace barriers, managers and an appropriate accommodation contact, with alternative contacts where safety or conflicts warrant it.
- **"CHRC must always come only after FPSLREB": false.** CHRC screening under section 41 considers whether other reasonably available processes ought to be used first; filing deadlines and circumstances matter.
- **"Disability is the only DTA ground": false.** CHRA protects 13 grounds.
- **"Closing a case means an employee received effective accommodation": unsupported.** The 2026 Auditor General audit measured administrative case-closure periods with significant data limitations.
