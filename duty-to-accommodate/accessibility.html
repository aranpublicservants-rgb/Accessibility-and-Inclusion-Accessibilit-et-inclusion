Accessibility and plain language

This independent research website aims to be usable by everyone, including people who use assistive technology, a keyboard, a phone or a screen magnifier.

Current status: improvements made, full conformance not yet established.

We have reviewed the code and strengthened important features. We have not yet completed end-to-end testing with assistive technologies and users with disabilities. We therefore do not claim full WCAG conformance.

Our design approach

We use WCAG 2.2 Level AA as our technical design goal. We also follow the Canada.ca Content Style Guide and plain-language guidance when writing and organizing the site. This independent project does not claim to be a Government of Canada website.

  • Use the language buttons to switch between English and French summaries.
  • Use the skip link to move to the main content.
  • Search and filter tables using labeled controls and your keyboard.
  • Read numerical findings as text without relying on charts or colour.
  • Use the source link in each record to check the original evidence.

Known limitations

  • Some original research documents are only available in English, and some external PDFs may not be fully accessible. We provide French summaries of our catalogue records, not translations of every original document.
  • Downloaded CSV files contain technical column names in English. The corresponding HTML tables provide a readable alternative.
  • French summaries are edited for clarity but have not yet had a separate professional translation review.
  • Automated syntax checks do not confirm real-world access. We still need keyboard, mobile zoom, screen reader and user testing on the published pages.

Terms used on this website

Duty to accommodate (DTA)

The legal duty to make reasonable changes so that a person can participate equally, up to undue hardship under the applicable human rights law.

Social model of disability

A way of thinking that looks at barriers in the workplace and how to remove them, instead of focusing only on a person's diagnosis.

Public Service Employee Survey (PSES)

A survey in which federal public servants report their workplace experiences. It shows patterns, not legal findings.

Case closure

The date an organization marks a request as closed. It does not always mean the employee received a complete or effective accommodation.

Provide feedback about accessibility

If you encounter a barrier on this website, send the page address, the problem and, if you wish, the technology you use. Do not include personal medical or accommodation-case details.
