01 / The usual route

What employees commonly do

This is a common pattern, not a required order. In some cases, a CHRC complaint, staffing complaint or workplace safety process may be appropriate earlier or at the same time. All options remain available to examine below.

1Try appropriate informal support

Consider talking with your supervisor or another manager, an accommodation centre, your union, the informal conflict management service or your organization's ombuds office. Describe the workplace barrier and the practical change you need. Ask for a written reply and an interim measure if delays cause harm.

Do not force a direct conversation with a manager you fear, particularly where there are safety or reprisal concerns. Informal support is not a legal prerequisite in every case.

2Consider a formal grievance

If the issue is unresolved and a grievance is available, check its deadline immediately. A grievance is a formal request for the employer to reconsider a workplace decision or action. Ask your union about representation and your collective agreement. Some employees can bring certain individual grievances without union representation.

If discrimination or disability is part of your concern, raise those human rights issues clearly in the available grievance process. Certain employer complaint policies may affect grievance rights; check before choosing.

3Board adjudication, if eligible

Some grievances that remain unresolved at the final employer level can be referred to the Federal Public Sector Labour Relations and Employment Board (FPSLREB). Not every grievance is eligible. Referral rules and bargaining-agent approval may apply. If the referred case raises the Canadian Human Rights Act, the party raising it must notify the CHRC under section 210 of the labour relations law.

Read the Board's grievance guide

Another possible route: human rights complaint

CHRC complaint — a separate possible route

If your concern involves discrimination covered by federal human rights law, the Canadian Human Rights Commission (CHRC) may be an option. It can decline to deal with a complaint when another reasonably available grievance or review process should be exhausted. The CHRC is a complaint-screening body, not an appeal tribunal for FPSLREB decisions. Depending on the case, a complaint may need to be filed before other processes end to preserve the usual 12-month deadline.

Read the CHRC complaint process

The CHRC is a separate route that may be relevant earlier. It is not a mandatory fourth step or an appeal after the Board. A complaint may be screened out while a reasonably available grievance process remains, and the usual 12-month CHRC limit still applies.

02 / Protect your rights

Watch the deadlines from the start

35days: general first-level individual grievance deadline under federal regulations, subject to applicable rules and exceptions.
40days: general period to refer an eligible grievance to adjudication after a final-level decision or the relevant response deadline. Check the applicable rules.
12months: CHRC's normal discrimination complaint filing period from the last act. Extensions are possible in limited circumstances; do not assume one will be granted.

The timelines above are general federal examples, not a personalized deadline calculation. Collective agreements, employers, procedural paths and exceptions can differ. Seek advice promptly.

The right is broader than disability

Accommodation can relate to any of 13 protected grounds

The Canadian Human Rights Act protects against discrimination based on 13 grounds. When a workplace rule creates a disadvantage linked to a protected ground, an employer may need to change the rule or working conditions, up to undue hardship. Accommodation does not always require a medical diagnosis.

All 13 grounds

Race; national or ethnic origin; colour; religion; age; sex (including pregnancy or childbirth); sexual orientation; gender identity or expression; marital status; family status; genetic characteristics; disability; and a conviction for which a pardon or record suspension was granted.

The 13 protected grounds — section 3

Examples other than disability

Religion: adjustments for sincerely held observances. Family status: adjustments for certain caregiving responsibilities. Sex: pregnancy and childbirth needs. Gender identity or expression: changing discriminatory workplace requirements. Race or national or ethnic origin: addressing discriminatory practices, including barriers affecting Indigenous employees.

These are illustrations, not automatic entitlements to a chosen arrangement. The facts and reasonable alternatives matter.

The information required depends on the request.

Federal guidance says many disability, family-status, religion and other accommodation requests can be resolved through dialogue without external supporting documents. A request can be verbal. Document the barrier and decisions to protect your rights.

Full list of routes

19 recourse and related options

All options are listed below, grouped by purpose, not in a required order. Some apply only to certain employees or issues. More than one may be relevant, but rules can limit duplicate proceedings.

Accommodation and informal support

1

Accommodation request or reconsideration

Start here for a needed change or to review a delayed, rejected or incomplete request.

What it may achieve: Reasoned decision, change in duties, schedule, workplace, equipment or procedure; possible temporary measures.

Official source
2

Union advice, ombuds, informal conflict management and mediation

Seek confidential advice, representation or a safer conversation. Not every concern should be raised directly with the manager involved.

What it may achieve: Explanation of rights, voluntary agreement, support with a request or referral. These options do not normally stop deadlines.

Official source

Grievances and review of decisions

3

Individual grievance

Challenge an employer decision or working condition when the federal grievance regime provides the right. Some forms require union support.

What it may achieve: An internal employer decision, reversal, accommodation or other relief; not every grievance is eligible for Board adjudication.

Official source
4

Group grievance, with bargaining-agent support

Two or more consenting employees in the same bargaining unit and portion of the federal administration are similarly affected by the interpretation or application of a collective agreement or arbitral award.

What it may achieve: A shared correction or binding interpretation for those covered, where the legal test is met; not a general class action.

Official source
5

Policy grievance, through the bargaining agent or employer

A collective agreement or arbitral award is being interpreted or applied in a manner affecting the bargaining unit or the parties. Employees cannot personally file this type.

What it may achieve: Interpretation of the agreement, systemic correction within the agreement, and orders within the adjudicator’s authority.

Official source
6

FPSLREB grievance adjudication

Where the final-level grievance is unresolved and meets the Board’s referral categories. Collective-agreement grievances require the specified union approval and representation.

What it may achieve: A binding decision and authorized remedies. The Board can apply human rights law and grant some human rights compensation when properly raised.

Official source
17

Judicial review, in limited cases

Where an administrative or tribunal decision may contain a legal or procedural error and judicial review is available. Seek legal advice about jurisdiction and short deadlines.

What it may achieve: A court may set aside a decision or send it back for reconsideration. Judicial review is not a routine merits appeal or a guarantee of a preferred accommodation.

Official source

Human rights, accessibility and privacy

7

CHRC human rights complaint; possible Canadian Human Rights Tribunal hearing

For alleged discrimination connected to a protected ground within federal jurisdiction. The CHRC may require a reasonably available grievance or review first, but this is not an automatic final step.

What it may achieve: A settlement, investigation or possible Tribunal referral. An upheld Tribunal complaint can lead to corrective orders, lost wages and expenses, plus statutory compensation caps for pain and suffering and wilful or reckless acts.

Official source
12

Accessible Canada Act feedback and Accessibility Commissioner complaint

Report disability-related barriers through an organization’s feedback process. Commissioner complaints are limited to harm from failures concerning accessibility plans, feedback processes and progress reports.

What it may achieve: Feedback-led improvements or accessibility enforcement within that limited mandate; not a general individual DTA grievance.

Official source
13

Privacy Act access/correction or Privacy Commissioner complaint

For inappropriate collection, use or disclosure of personal or medical information by a covered federal institution; or refusal to provide or correct personal information.

What it may achieve: Records access, correction, investigation or recommendations, depending on the law; not a general award for accommodation denial.

Official source
14

Official languages complaint

For infringements of rights under the Official Languages Act, including language-of-work rights where they apply. Not every language-related accommodation issue is an Official Languages Act complaint.

What it may achieve: An investigation, recommendation and applicable statutory relief through the language-rights regime.

Official source

Staffing, safety and protected processes

8

FPSLREB staffing complaint

Certain eligible internal appointment, layoff, revocation or corrective-action complaints, including abuse of authority. The usual appointment deadline is 15 calendar days after relevant final notice.

What it may achieve: Reassessment, revocation or other authorized corrective measure. For appointment complaints the Board cannot order you directly appointed.

Official source
9

Public Service Commission or deputy-head staffing investigation

Specific allegations of fraud, political influence, external-appointment irregularities or improper conduct in delegated internal staffing. Jurisdiction varies by allegation.

What it may achieve: Investigation and corrective staffing measures, potentially revocation, within the responsible authority’s powers.

Official source
10

Workplace harassment and violence occurrence process

For conduct meeting the federal workplace harassment and violence definition. Notify the employer or designated recipient; negotiated resolution, voluntary conciliation or an investigation may follow.

What it may achieve: Preventive safety measures, resolution agreement, investigation findings and recommendations. The process does not itself award human rights damages.

Official source
11

Occupational health and safety complaint or reprisal

When there is an occupational hazard, an employer’s failure to comply with health-and-safety obligations, or reprisal for exercising protected safety rights. Different Labour Program or Board rules may apply.

What it may achieve: Inspection, compliance direction, preventive change or eligible reprisal remedy; not an automatic accommodation payout.

Official source
15

Unfair labour practice complaint

For specific violations of federal labour relations duties, such as interference with protected union activities. Not a general DTA remedy.

What it may achieve: Board orders within labour-relations jurisdiction.

Official source
16

Public Sector Integrity Commissioner reprisal complaint

Only where reprisal is linked to a protected disclosure of wrongdoing or participation in its investigation. The filing deadline is generally 60 days and procedural restrictions apply.

What it may achieve: Investigation and possible Public Servants Disclosure Protection Tribunal remedy. Not every reprisal for an accommodation request qualifies.

Official source

Work injury and benefit claims

18

Work-related injury or illness: workers’ compensation claim and appeal

Where a workplace injury or illness is involved, eligible federal employees may claim benefits through the Government Employees Compensation Act and the responsible provincial workers’ compensation board. Reporting and appeal deadlines differ. This is distinct from, but may overlap with, the employer’s duty to accommodate.

What it may achieve: Medical or rehabilitation benefits, wage replacement, injury-on-duty leave where eligible, support for safe return to work and review or appeal of a compensation decision.

Official compensation and appeal information
19

Disability insurance or benefit-plan appeal

If a disability insurance or benefits claim is denied or stopped, the plan may have its own appeal process and deadlines. This is separate from workplace accommodation: a benefits denial does not determine whether the employer met its duty to accommodate.

What it may achieve: Reconsideration or payment of eligible benefits, depending on the plan rules and evidence. Check the plan administrator and bargaining agent or compensation advisor.

Federal employee wellness and benefits guidance

04 / Other circumstances

Some concerns need a different route

Staffing and assessment accommodations

If the issue is an internal appointment or staffing assessment, a staffing-specific complaint or an investigation route may apply. Eligibility and deadlines depend on the appointment process. Some Board staffing complaints have a 15-day filing period after the final notice. Do not send everyone through the ordinary workplace grievance flow.

FPSLREB staffing complaints information

Harassment and violence, including disability-related conduct

If the situation meets the definition of workplace harassment and violence, the occurrence-resolution process under federal health and safety law may apply. This process has different purposes and procedures from a grievance or human rights complaint. Safety and urgency matter; informal discussion with the person involved need not be the first step.

Federal harassment and violence prevention guidance

Union status, EX positions or a different federal employer

Collective agreement coverage, managerial or confidential status, the employer's legislation and which Board has jurisdiction can change the available route. For grievances about collective agreement provisions, bargaining-agent approval and representation are required. Employees outside a bargaining unit may still have certain grievance rights; do not assume all matters can be referred to Board adjudication.

The other process is unavailable, inadequate or taking years

The CHRC considers whether another procedure is reasonably available, not just whether it exists on paper. When grievance or review processes are unavailable or unreasonably delayed, the CHRC may consider the complaint. This is case-specific; do not treat a delay as automatic permission to bypass an available process. Protect any applicable filing deadlines.
