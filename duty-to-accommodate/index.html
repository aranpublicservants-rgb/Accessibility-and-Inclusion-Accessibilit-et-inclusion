01 / Strengths and weaknesses

What the available evidence shows

Evidence is strongest when plans, employees' reported experiences and independent operational records are read together. Some organizations show promising approaches; no single indicator establishes a complete or effective accommodation.

Promising practice

Faster processing where timeliness is monitored

The Auditor General found average case-closure times of 24–81 days in five organizations with tracking or informal timeliness goals, compared with 169–310 days in the two without. This association is not proof of causation.

View audit comparison
Documented weakness

Incomplete records hide systemic problems

The audit found inconsistent recording of requests and limited capacity to detect recurring barriers. Some requests handled solely by supervisors may not appear in central systems.

Review audit limitations
Promising practice

Barrier-first, portable solutions

The GC Workplace Accessibility Passport is voluntary and helps record agreed supports across jobs. Updated manager guidance generally encourages relying on an existing Passport rather than requesting the same documentation again. SSC's adaptive-technology program was recognized in the audit.

Documented weakness

People may avoid requesting what they need

In the 2025 Staffing and Non-Partisanship Survey, 10% of employees with disabilities who needed assessment accommodation did not request it. Of that group, 70% worried that asking would reduce their chances of success.

Read staffing survey ↗
Promising practice

Concrete commitments with owners and measures

Recent Public Service Commission and PSPC accessibility plans describe standardized tracking, clearer responsibilities, service standards or measurable actions. These are promising plans—not evidence yet that the changes improved 2024 outcomes.

Documented weakness

Harm, low trust and inconsistent consultation

In the supplied PSES comparison, 56 of 56 organizations with comparable results show more reported discrimination among employees with disabilities. The 2026 audit also found that disability networks were not consistently consulted on relevant decisions.

What the audit adds

Beyond employee perception

Auditors examined request-handling systems, engagement with disability networks and organizational practices, providing a different kind of evidence from employee survey percentages.

Questions organizations should answer

  • How long until an effective measure is in place—not just case closure?
  • Are interim accommodations offered while requests are assessed?
  • Are informal and manager-led requests counted consistently?
  • Are disability networks involved in decisions and evaluation?

04 / Social model in practice

What would good accommodation delivery look like?

The social model shifts attention from 'fixing the person' to removing barriers in systems, practices and environments. This is a suggested evidence checklist, not an official legal certification framework.

01Identify the barrier

Describe what prevents equitable participation, rather than asking employees to disclose unnecessary diagnosis details.

02Act promptly

Track request dates, interim supports and time to effective resolution, not only case-closure dates.

03Make support portable

Respect agreed solutions across teams and jobs and avoid unnecessary repeated assessments.

04Co-design with employees

Engage employees and disability networks in design, implementation and review of systemic changes.

05Measure outcomes fairly

Report timeliness, satisfaction of all requesters, unresolved needs and patterns without exposing personal information.

06Prevent future barriers

Use recurring evidence to improve staffing, hybrid work, procurement, digital tools and occupational health and safety.

Legal duties remain distinct

The Canadian Human Rights Act underpins individualized accommodation up to undue hardship under applicable legal standards. The Accessible Canada Act focuses on identifying, removing and preventing accessibility barriers, including plans and progress reporting. The Canada Labour Code Part II and harassment-and-violence prevention rules impose other preventive duties where applicable. These frameworks can overlap but are not interchangeable.

Additional data worth collecting

Current published datasets rarely provide comparable organization-level figures for effective completion time, interim measures, repeated medical documentation, manager-only requests, denials, appeals, portability after transfers and unmet accommodation needs. Publish these as 'not available' rather than guessing.

Methodology and sources. 2024 PSES figures and plan classifications originate from two supplied compiled CSV files, supplemented by independently verified official audit and survey sources. Most PSES percentages are rounded; denominators vary and suppression occurs for small groups. The 92-organization dataset is not a census of every federal entity. Audit averages use unlike administrative datasets. The social-model classification records literal mentions in selected documents, not a verified grade of organizational practice. Full plan/progress-report recoding and more recent employee outcome data are needed before drawing stronger comparisons. Dataset snapshot: November 2026.
