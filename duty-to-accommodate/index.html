Duty to accommodate: understand the evidence and your next steps

The duty to accommodate is about removing workplace barriers linked to protected human rights grounds. It is not limited to disability. This site explains the evidence and how employees can request and follow up on an accommodation.

Section 2 of 2 · Employee guide

Need an accommodation? Here is what to do.

The first step is normally to raise the workplace barrier with your manager or your organization’s accommodation contact—not to file a complaint. If that is unsafe or inappropriate, use another designated contact or get union advice. The steps below reflect official federal guidance.

This right is not limited to disability.

Federal human rights law protects against discrimination on 13 grounds. Accommodation may be required for disability, religion, family responsibilities, pregnancy or other protected grounds when a workplace rule creates a barrier. The employer must consider reasonable measures up to undue hardship. A preferred arrangement is not automatically required if another effective and reasonable solution is offered.

You do not have to self-identify for employment equity to be eligible for accommodation. A request may be made verbally. The employer should assess your actual needs rather than treating an accommodation request as an ordinary work-location preference.

See all 13 protected human rights grounds

The Canadian Human Rights Act lists: race; national or ethnic origin; colour; religion; age; sex (including pregnancy and childbirth); sexual orientation; gender identity or expression; marital status; family status; genetic characteristics; disability; and conviction for an offence for which a pardon or record suspension was granted.

Accommodation depends on whether a real workplace barrier affects someone because of a protected ground. Different grounds may overlap. The federal manager guide says the process applies to people employed in temporary, term, casual and student roles within its defined scope, as well as candidates during hiring processes.

Follow the workplace accommodation process

This is the usual practical process, not a rigid legal checklist. Your organization may have its own form or specialized contact.

  1. 1

    Identify the barrier and make your request

    Explain which work task, rule or environment is creating a barrier, and what change could help. Usually start with your manager or designated workplace accommodation service. You may make a verbal request; keeping a written record can help.

    You do not need to know the legal terminology to raise a need. A manager may have to enquire when circumstances suggest an accommodation is required.

  2. 2

    Discuss solutions and share only relevant information

    Work with the manager to understand the barrier and assess practical options. The employer may need information about your functional needs and may seek more evidence when reasonably required. A diagnosis is not automatically necessary for every request. Medical details should be limited to what is needed and handled confidentially.

    The process should protect your dignity and privacy. Accommodation documents should be kept confidential and separate from ordinary personnel files.

  3. 3

    Ask about a temporary measure while the request is reviewed

    If gathering information or buying equipment takes time, discuss interim adjustments. These might include accessible documents, different hours, temporary equipment or a suitable work location, depending on the facts.

    A flexible work arrangement alone is not necessarily a legal accommodation. The decision should address the actual barrier and protected need.

  4. 4

    Get the decision and implementation plan

    Ask what has been approved, when it starts and who will arrange it. If the request is refused or changed, ask for the reasons and what alternatives were considered. The manager should communicate the decision promptly and provide reasons when accommodation is not offered.

  5. 5

    Check whether the accommodation works

    Agree on a review date. If the measure is ineffective, circumstances change or you move roles, ask to reassess it. For employees with disabilities, the voluntary GC Workplace Accessibility Passport can help record agreed supports and reduce repeated explanations.

    The Passport is optional and is designed for disability-related barriers; it is not a mandatory form for every protected ground.

Official basis: Treasury Board’s five-stage process for managers and the CHRC’s workplace accommodation guide. These steps are adapted for employees in plain language.

Medical notes and supporting information: what employees need to know

This is often the most confusing part of a disability-related accommodation request. Federal guidance distinguishes between a simple discussion, a medical note to confirm an absence, and information about a person's ability to do particular work tasks. These are not interchangeable.

Start with the question: what information is actually needed?

Treasury Board says the type, amount and source of supporting information must be considered case by case, regardless of the protected ground. Many requests can be settled with the employee through discussion. If information is needed, it should concern the workplace barrier and functional abilities, not a general medical investigation.

Official Treasury Board information requirements

Medical Absence Form

Purpose: to confirm that illness or injury justified time away from work. It can confirm dates and whether a follow-up assessment is needed. It is not a form for deciding all workplace accommodation requirements.

Federal disability-management guidance says fees for this form are normally the employee’s responsibility under the collective-agreement language it describes. Check current applicable terms.

View official absence form

Functional Abilities Form

Purpose: to identify abilities, restrictions and limitations relevant to staying at work or returning safely. The employer should describe the actual work demands; a practitioner provides work-related functional information.

Federal guidance recommends that departments consider reimbursing fees for this form. That is not the same as an unconditional entitlement to reimbursement; ask who will authorize and pay before the appointment.

View official abilities form
When can a manager request a doctor's note?

Not automatically for every accommodation. An employee may describe a barrier and agree on an adjustment without outside documents. When a disability-related restriction or safety issue cannot be assessed with available information, the employer may reasonably ask for focused confirmation from an appropriate health professional. Sick-leave certification is a different question governed by applicable leave rules.

The CHRC encourages the least intrusive approach and is open to relevant information from appropriately qualified professionals such as nurse practitioners, occupational therapists, physiotherapists, psychologists, chiropractors or midwives, depending on the question. A family physician is not the only potential source.

CHRC medical-information guidance
What should a practitioner be asked to explain?

The official forms and CHRC guidance emphasize work-related questions. Useful topics may include:

  • Which essential duties or work conditions are affected, and how?
  • What abilities, restrictions or limitations must be considered?
  • Are restrictions temporary, recurring or long-term, and when should they be reviewed?
  • What adjustments could permit the work to be done safely, and is a gradual return needed?
  • For genuinely safety-sensitive work, is there a specific work-related safety restriction?
Before seeing a health practitioner

Ask the department what exact document is required and why. Request a description of the essential tasks and conditions that the practitioner should assess. Confirm the deadline, who can receive the information securely, and whether the fee will be reimbursed. Bring existing relevant documentation, if any. These are practical preparation steps drawn from the federal forms process, not a mandatory legal checklist.

The manager should provide relevant job duties and work conditions to the practitioner. A note stating only that an employee 'must work from home' may not explain the actual functional restriction or whether other measures would meet the need. The employer should seek clarification where reasonably required, not dismiss the need without assessment.

Federal practitioner-information guide
Can a manager demand my diagnosis, medications or complete medical file?

Usually not. Treasury Board's 2025 accommodation-specific privacy notice says managers may request what is needed about functional limitations and safety, but are not entitled to exact diagnoses or prescribed treatments. The CHRC recognizes exceptional safety-related circumstances. Even if an employee volunteers detailed information, the employer must protect it and limit its use and disclosure.

TBS privacy notice, question 3
What if I already supplied a note or have an Accessibility Passport?

Treasury Board guidance says previous information, including an existing GC Workplace Accessibility Passport, should normally be used where adequate. A change of manager or senior leadership alone does not normally justify fresh external medical evidence, reassessment or withdrawal of agreed supports. New information may be appropriate where job duties or relevant circumstances changed, or existing evidence no longer answers the required work-related question.

TBS guidance on earlier documentation
What if the employer requests an independent medical examination?

An independent medical evaluation is not the usual first step. The CHRC identifies narrow situations where it may be considered after clarification of existing information, or where a contract, law or worker-initiated specialist request provides a basis. The Commission advises caution, appropriate consent, a mutually acceptable professional where feasible, and employer-funded examination costs. Ask for the legal or contractual basis, the exact questions, how the information will be protected, and union or qualified advice before proceeding.

A separate federal occupational-health evaluation program also exists. Health Canada's current program page says its Public Service Occupational Health Program does not perform fitness-to-work evaluations as part of disability-management processes. Do not assume an older departmental handbook describes current service availability.

CHRC: independent medical evaluations · Health Canada: current program scope
What if I have no family doctor or cannot pay for a note?

Tell the accommodation contact and ask whether an existing assessment, workplace specialist or another regulated practitioner can supply sufficient information. The CHRC encourages flexibility when workers lack a family doctor. Before paying, ask which form is actually required, whether the employer will pay or reimburse the fee, and how much time you have. The two federal sample forms have different fee guidance, so reimbursement must not be assumed.

Federal guidance on forms and fees

Religion and other protected grounds

A religious accommodation may require information describing a sincerely held belief or practice, but a medical certificate is generally unrelated. The employer should assess the real workplace conflict, not require evidence designed for disability claims.

Family status and caregiving

An employee may need to explain an actual caregiving obligation, conflict with work requirements and reasonably available alternatives. The evidence will depend on the circumstances; a medical note is not a universal prerequisite for family-status requests.

What if the illness or injury happened at work?

The federal sample Medical Absence Form expressly says it is not intended for work-related illness or injury. It points to the relevant provincial or territorial workers’ compensation physician report. A workers’ compensation process and a workplace accommodation request can relate to the same situation but have different forms, purposes and decision-makers.

Official absence form: work-injury exception
Is an insurance medical form the same as an accommodation note?

No. Disability insurance and benefit plans may require information to decide eligibility under their own plan definitions. Workplace accommodation asks what barriers and restrictions must be addressed so work can be done. The information needed, the decision-makers and privacy rules may differ. Ask which process is requesting a document before supplying it.

Federal disability-management handbook

Forms and consent are separate documents. Treasury Board guidance says a manager may contact a health practitioner only with the employee’s consent. The federal consent-form example authorizes targeted clarification of functional limitations, not unlimited access to medical history.

Privacy and confidentiality: what must be protected?

Accommodation records may reveal sensitive health, disability, family or religious information. Federal departments need enough information to make a decision, but they must limit collection, sharing and access. A manager receiving private information does not make it ordinary personnel information.

A specific federal privacy rule applies to accommodation requests.

Treasury Board’s Privacy Implementation Notice 2022–03, revised effective May 27, 2025, covers institutions subject to its duty-to-accommodate framework. It addresses legal authority, the PSE 907 personal information bank, committee access, secure storage, decisions, records, privacy breaches and small-group reporting. Its application is defined in the notice; separate employers may have different applicable instruments.

Read the complete Treasury Board privacy notice

Collect only what is needed

Explain the purpose of each requested document. Ask about functional limitations and workplace effects rather than diagnosis, medications or treatment records, subject to narrow safety exceptions.

Limit who can see it

Only personnel who need the information for their assigned role should receive it. If a committee reviews a request, membership and identifying details should be minimized; de-identification is encouraged where feasible.

Secure the accommodation file

Treasury Board recommends treating these sensitive records generally as Protected B, with access controls and safeguards matching that classification. Keep accommodation material confidential and separate from ordinary HR records. Avoid uncontrolled email attachments and broad-access team folders.

Protect the right to access and correct records

Employees may seek access to their accommodation decisions and related personal information, subject to legal exceptions, and ask for correction. The employer should record decisions even when they followed an informal discussion. Federal privacy regulations generally require keeping personal information used in decisions for at least two years after last use, with exceptions.

Can the manager share my information with coworkers or a committee?

Not simply because other people are curious or affected by scheduling. The employer should communicate only what others need to implement the solution; it does not ordinarily need to disclose the protected ground or diagnosis. A formal accommodation review committee must restrict membership and access to those with a legitimate need, and consider removing identifying details where possible. Some disclosures can be permitted by law, so confidentiality is not absolute.

TBS: committee and use-of-information rules
What if my accommodation details were shared incorrectly?

Record what was disclosed, when and to whom. Report the concern through the institution’s designated privacy-breach or ATIP contact, using a secure channel. Ask how the information will be contained and whether you will be notified of the investigation and measures. Privacy Act complaint processes may also be relevant. Do not copy or distribute more sensitive information to prove the problem.

The 2025 Treasury Board notice recommends treating accommodation breaches as potentially material because of sensitivity and possible harm. It also warns that de-identified data can sometimes re-identify employees in small groups.

Justice Canada’s 2023–24 Privacy Act annual report says that, in September 2023, an employee improperly accessed duty-to-accommodate files in a Digital Workspace. The department reported access to 106 documents concerning more than 74 employees and 12 downloaded documents concerning nine employees. Affected individuals were notified. This illustrates the practical danger of overbroad access; it does not show that every department has the same controls.

If the response is delayed, denied or not working

Do not assume that you must begin with an ombuds office or a human rights complaint. Choose the next step that fits what has happened, and protect any formal deadline.

1. Ask for a clear response or reconsideration

Follow up in writing with the person responsible. Ask what is still needed, who decides, when you will hear back, and whether an interim measure can be put in place. If refused, ask for written reasons and alternatives. Keep dates and copies of correspondence.

2. Get support from the right internal contact

Contact your department’s workplace accommodation centre or HR functional specialist. Your union can explain the collective agreement, deadlines and possible grievance rights. If the manager is the problem, use another appropriate manager or designated contact. Informal conflict management or an ombuds office may help, but they are optional supports, not required first doors.

3. Check formal options and deadlines promptly

A formal grievance may be available for an unresolved workplace decision. Ask your union or an adviser about eligibility and timing. A grievance may reach the Federal Public Sector Labour Relations and Employment Board only where the law permits adjudication. You can also consider a CHRC discrimination complaint when a protected ground is involved; the CHRC may ask that another reasonably available grievance or review process be used first. It is not an appeal body for the Board.

In the common federal public service grievance regime, the initial individual-grievance deadline is generally 35 days. The CHRC’s normal complaint period is 12 months. Other processes have different deadlines. Informal discussions do not automatically pause those clocks. Get timely advice about your specific employer and circumstances.

Human rights screening, section 41 · Grievance filing periods

4. A different procedure may apply to the actual problem

If the barrier happened in a staffing competition or assessment, check staffing-specific complaint or investigation rules immediately. If the conduct involves workplace harassment and violence, the federal occurrence process may apply. A privacy complaint may be relevant if sensitive information was mishandled. These are distinct from an ordinary accommodation request. For comprehensive information on all employee recourse routes, use a dedicated recourse resource rather than treating this DTA process as a universal legal pathway.

What if the employer says the adjustment is too costly?

The employer has a legal duty up to undue hardship. Federal guidance identifies cost, health and safety as the relevant hardship factors; inconvenience or a manager’s small operating budget is not, on its own, proof. An employer asserting undue hardship should have objective evidence and examine realistic alternatives. Ask for the written reasons, alternatives considered and what temporary measures remain possible. A specific case still requires an individualized assessment.

TBS: evidence of undue hardship
What if the barrier involves on-site work or telework?

The 2026 federal on-site-presence guidance expressly preserves the duty to accommodate and requires case-by-case review of barriers linked to protected grounds. Describe which worksite conditions create the barrier and what functional restriction results. Telework may be one reasonable accommodation, but it is not automatically required simply because requested. Ask the employer to assess effective alternatives and give reasons for its decision.

Current federal on-site presence and DTA guidance
What if a workplace barrier is being treated as poor performance?

Treasury Board guidance warns that an employee who has not been reasonably accommodated may appear to be underperforming. Managers may have a duty to enquire when circumstances indicate a possible accommodation need, while respecting privacy and avoiding assumptions. Ask to discuss the work barrier and appropriate measures, and document the discussion. Accommodation does not necessarily remove legitimate essential job requirements.

TBS: performance and the duty to accommodate
Protect your deadlines.

An internal discussion, informal conflict management, a request for reconsideration or an ombuds consultation does not necessarily extend the time to file a grievance or complaint. Seek advice promptly if a formal deadline may be approaching.

Laws, Treasury Board directives and official guidance

These instruments have different legal effects. A statute creates enforceable rights and duties; a Treasury Board policy or directive applies to the organizations within its stated scope; a guide explains implementation and good practice. No single document replaces the others. Check current collective-agreement and departmental instructions as well.
